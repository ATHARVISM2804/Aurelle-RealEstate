# Turn 3D Browsing Into Scored, Bookable Intent

The most valuable problem for developers and brokerages in India and Dubai is not getting leads. Leads are cheap. The money is lost after the click. Teams pay for Meta leads they cannot qualify. They answer late, on the wrong channel, from staff phones that never sync to a CRM. Then they cannot show whether digital or a broker produced the booking. That last question matters a lot, because broker commission costs **1-3% of sale value in India and 4-6% (at peaks 10-12%) in Dubai**, which is far more per booking than any CPL saving. On the funnel ranges available, a ₹500 Meta lead becomes roughly a **₹1 lakh paid-media booking**. A UAE resident's AED 80 lead becomes an **AED 1,300-2,200 site visit**, because only about 2-6% of raw leads ever visit in either market. The product should therefore not be "a 3D website plus a chatbot plus a CRM." It should be one **intent-and-attribution pipeline**:

- The 3D site and unit explorer are the instruments that capture intent.
- The WhatsApp bot answers in seconds from a locked project fact base and asks the qualifying questions.
- The CRM layer turns behaviour and answers into a score.
- It sends qualified and site-visit events back to Meta.
- It timestamps who introduced each buyer.

The best first market is mid-size and new **Dubai off-plan developers**: 258 developers ran 648 launches in 2025, about 40% of buyers come from overseas, and incumbent developer CRMs are weak. Indian premium developers selling to NRIs come second. Pricing should be per launch, and proof should be stated as cost per site visit. One caveat runs through the whole report. **Almost every funnel number in these markets comes from vendors or agencies selling a fix.** The only platform-grade evidence is Meta's own A/B test of CRM feedback and old US speed-to-lead studies. The founder must validate the core assumptions in customer interviews before committing the roadmap.

## Leads are cheap; the losses happen after the click

First, a word on evidence, since it shapes every recommendation. No independent audited study of Indian or UAE real estate lead funnels was found. CPL, junk-lead share, lead-to-visit and visit-to-booking figures all come from proptech vendors (Sell.Do, Kolonell, Leadproio, opZynic) or ad agencies. The reliable data covers market structure instead: DLD broker counts, ANAROCK sales volumes, listed-developer disclosures and regulator texts. Below, figures are labelled **independent**, **platform-reported** (Meta, Zillow, NAR, Property Finder, ANAROCK) or **vendor claim**. The widely repeated "70-90% junk leads" figure could not be traced to any source and should not be used.

| Rank | Problem | Why it is high-value | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Booking attribution and broker cost | Broker commission (India 1-3%, Dubai 4-6%+) dwarfs paid-media cost per booking; developers cannot prove which channel sourced a sale | Commission rates: press and recruiter data; attribution pain: vendor |
| 2 | Cheap but unqualified Meta leads | Cost per visit runs 15-40x CPL; teams optimise to CPL and pay for unreachable numbers | Vendor and agency only; no credible junk % |
| 3 | Slow, office-hours-only first response | Ads are consumed evenings and weekends; NRI and overseas buyers enquire in other time zones | US studies (independent but old); local figures vendor-only |
| 4 | WhatsApp as a data black hole plus agent churn | First contact happens on WhatsApp on staff phones; Dubai agents leave within about 6 months, taking leads with them | Portal claim, recruiter data |
| 5 | Channel-partner disputes and payouts | Retrospective claims, duplicate leads, 60-90+ day payouts, CPs deprioritising slow payers | Vendor claims; court ruling on Dubai registration |
| 6 | Trust and remote buying | Render-vs-reality complaints, stale "sold" inventory, Facebook-ad fraud warnings, overseas buyers visiting half as often | Press, regulators, buyer reviews (directional) |
| 7 | Site-visit no-shows and scheduling | The visit is the conversion event and the moment ownership is contested | Vendor only; no verified no-show data |

**Attribution is the biggest prize because commission, not media, is the largest acquisition cost.** Channel partners source an estimated **40-70% of primary residential sales in Indian metros** and **50-60% in MMR**, where **the top 5% of CPs generate 40-50% of bookings** ([GHAR.tv](https://www.ghar.tv/intelligence/india-real-estate-broker-channel-partner-industry-report-2026/artgi414); [Sell.Do](https://www.sell.do/blog/channel-partner-management-for-real-estate-developers-2026)). Primary brokerage runs 1-3% of booking value. At Godrej's FY25 average ticket of about ₹1.9 crore (₹29,444 crore across 15,302 homes), that is roughly ₹2-6 lakh per CP-sourced sale ([SME Street](https://smestreet.in/sectors/godrej-properties-reports-record-breaking-fy25-bookings-of-inr-29444-cr-9029724)). In Dubai, recruiter data puts off-plan developer commission at **4-6%** ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/10/30/dubai-real-estate-agents-salaries/)). In the 2024 peak, private developers paid **10-12%, and up to 15% on bulk deals**, plus gold-bar launch incentives ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/in-dubai-property-broker-commissions-rise-further---some-are-getting-gold-too-1.1718164344640)). On an AED 1.8M unit, 4-6% is AED 72,000-108,000. One vendor's modelled cost per digital sale is about AED 20,900 ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/real-estate-developer-website-lead-capture-dubai-2026)).

The disputes are structural. A Dubai Court of Appeal held that an **unregistered brokerage agreement is void for commission purposes** ([Mondaq/BSA](https://mondaq.com/real-estate/1507012/brokers-commission-and-the-registration-of-the-agreement-with-dubai-land-department)), so timestamped introduction records carry legal weight. In India, the typical dispute is a buyer who registers directly in January, visits with CP-A in March and books with CP-B in May ([Sell.Do](https://www.sell.do/blog/channel-partner-management-for-real-estate-developers-2026)). Vendor claims that **15-20% of CP leads are disputed** and that CPs wait **60-90+ days** for payouts have no stated method ([opZynic](https://www.opzynic.com/blog/real-estate-channel-partner-program-india)), but every source describes the same pattern.

**Meta lead quality is the most visible complaint, but the evidence says "unreachable and unqualified", not "fake".** An Indian agency founder describes leads as "usually unqualified rather than fake" ([Leadproio](https://leadproio.com/real-estate-cost-per-lead-india/)). One audit anecdote found that **380 of 400 Meta form leads never picked up**, and nobody was tracking cost per site visit ([Shreyas Bagal](https://shreyasbagal.in/facebook-lead-ads-guide)). In Dubai, a brokerage's AED 180 form-fill "looked efficient on a dashboard". After adding qualifying questions, CPL rose to AED 310 with better leads ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/lead-generation-agency-dubai-quote-traps-2026)). Brokers and CPs also fill developer forms to collect intel and "then just vanish into thin air" ([aajneeti.social](https://aajneeti.social/lead-excuse-is-killing-indian-real-estate-growth)). The developer-side response is often just to label the lead "fake" and move on.

**Response speed is the most controllable leak.** Telecallers earn ₹12,000-28,000 a month in base pay and work fixed shifts ([Jobkar](https://jobkar.in/blog/telecaller-jobs-india-salary-guide)). A lead arriving at 9:47 PM is typically called the next morning ([Sell.Do](https://www.sell.do/blog/speed-to-lead-real-estate-2026)). Dubai vendors claim average response times of **4-14 hours**, with no disclosed method ([Merkz Digital](https://merkzdigital.com/blog/lead-follow-up-speed-dubai-real-estate)). Buyer reviews say the same thing in plain words: "of 10 different enquiries I make, I get only one agent responding" ([Trustpilot Bayut](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/bayut.com)).

The channel makes it worse. Property Finder says **60-80% of seekers make their first enquiry on WhatsApp** (platform claim, unsourced) ([Property Finder Partner Hub](https://propertyfinder.ae/partnerhub/?p=134)). Those chats live on agents' phones. Dubai recruiters report **average agent tenure has fallen from about 12 months to 6 or fewer**, with about 37 new agents entering daily ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/10/30/dubai-real-estate-agents-salaries/)). A brokerage can therefore lose most of its frontline, and the pipeline in their phones, every year.

**Trust is a conversion constraint, not a branding nicety.** About **40% of Dubai buyers are from overseas** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-property-agents-under-pressure-as-real-estate-market-heats-up-in-2025-1.500167818)). In India, **NRIs rose from 5% to 23% of DLF's sales between FY22 and FY24** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/rupee-fall-boosts-nri-investments-in-luxury-and-ultra-luxury-properties-125030900443_1.html)). Dubai Police have warned the public against buying property via Facebook ads ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/dubai-police-warn-against-buying-or-renting-property-via-facebook-ads-1.63177687)). Buyers complain about bait listings, "already sold" units and prices "30-40 percent higher when you contact the agents" ([Trustpilot Bayut](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/bayut.com)). Gurugram buyers were shown "a single gated community" that turned out to be split by public roads ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/gurugram-homebuyers-m3m-builder/2856260)). Indian portal users report, second-hand via a portal-comparison blog, "50+ spam calls in 3 days" after a single enquiry ([Beegru](https://beegru.com/blog/priya-ramachandran/top-10-real-estate-websites-in-india-2026-beegru)). This is why buyers give fake numbers in the first place. The existing Aurelle form's promise, "We never share your details," answers a documented pain and should become a headline.

Treat the low cost-per-booking claims in circulation as cherry-picked. Examples include "₹5,581 per confirmed booking" ([Clutch: Realtica](https://clutch.co/profile/realtica)) and portal bookings at ₹3,500-9,000. They contradict the CPL and conversion ranges above.

**The response-time evidence is strong in direction and weak in locality.** The InsideSales Lead Response Management study (2007, vendor-funded, 15,000+ non-real-estate leads) found **100x higher odds of contact and 21x higher odds of qualification at 5 minutes versus 30**. It measured qualification, not sales; the popular "21x more likely to close" is a misquotation ([real-estate.ca](https://www.real-estate.ca/blog/the-lead-response-management-study)). HBR's 2011 audit of 2,241 firms found replies within an hour were **nearly 7x more likely** to produce a qualifying conversation ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)). The most relevant recent number is from Zillow's 2025 consumer survey: **47% of buyers hire the first agent they speak with**, and half prefer messaging over calls ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/why-most-buyers-hire-the-first-agent-they-talk-to/)). Together these support a sub-minute WhatsApp first touch. They do not prove a booking uplift; no India or UAE study links response time to bookings. The India-specific "23% conversation rate within a minute vs under 5% after hours" is an unattributed vendor figure ([Sell.Do](https://www.sell.do/blog/speed-to-lead-real-estate-2026)).

**The evidence on 3D is weaker than the founder probably hopes, and it points to a different value.** The best study is published by Matterport: a Texas Tech regression on 143,575 US MLS listings found 3D-tour homes sold for **4-9% more and up to 31% faster** ([Matterport](https://matterport.com/blog/3d-tours-properties-sell-31-faster-and-higher-price)). Those were resale homes with likely selection bias, so it says nothing direct about off-plan. Buyer surveys consistently rank **floor plans above virtual tours**. In NAR's 2025 survey, 57% rated floor plans very useful against 41% for virtual tours ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-04/2025-home-buyers-and-sellers-generational-trends-04-01-2025.pdf)). Zillow finds most buyers are more likely to view a home whose floor plan they like, and more than half regret visits they would have skipped after seeing the plan ([Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/pro/agents-add-value/)). The plausible mechanism is **self-selection**: buyers who explore a unit and still ask for a visit are better leads. That makes the explorer a qualification instrument and a trust tool. Its job is not just to impress. No study measures this for interactive unit selectors on off-plan projects. Kolonell's claim that live inventory pages get forms filled "twice as often" as a PDF brochure is a vendor claim ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/real-estate-developer-website-lead-capture-dubai-2026)).

**Every chatbot performance number is a vendor claim; the independent evidence is about risk.** Claims include Bren's "+35% qualified leads" ([Perimattic](https://perimattic.com/case-studies/ai-chatbot-solution-for-major-real-estate-company/)), a bot booking "65% of weekend site visits" ([GInfomedia](https://ginfomedia.in/whatsapp-ai-chatbot-for-lead-generation)) and Lodha's "20x ROI" with Haptik ([Haptik](https://www.haptik.ai/blog/real-estate-ai-agents-property-discovery-sales-support)). None is audited. On the other side, Gartner found **64% of customers would prefer companies not use AI in service** ([ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/your-customers-arent-keen-on-that-customer-service-chatbot-you-introduced-heres-why)). *Moffatt v. Air Canada* held a company liable for its chatbot's wrong answer ([VKTR](https://www.vktr.com/customer-experience/exploring-air-canadas-ai-chatbot-dilemma/)). For a bot quoting prices, availability and possession dates, that precedent is the design brief. The defensible case for the bot is coverage and speed with a human always one tap away. Replacing sales is not the case.

Three conclusions follow.

First, **a WhatsApp bot or an AI score alone is not defensible**. BSPs sell bots for ₹1,000-8,000 a month. Sell.Do and Leadrat already sell AI scoring and voice agents as usage add-ons, so they can match a stand-alone feature quickly.

Second, the defensible asset is **first-party behavioural intent that only the project site generates**. That means which unit, which floor and view, the price reveal, the time spent in the 3D plan and repeat visits, joined to the WhatsApp conversation and pushed into a score. No vendor found does this credibly. VirtualHomes claims to "predict likelihood of sale" but shows no evidence.

Third, **the UAE developer segment has the weakest CRM incumbent**. PropSpace is broker- and listing-centric, while India has a strong developer CRM in Sell.Do. The documented user complaints in India are add-on cost creep, integration work and adoption friction rather than missing features. Sell.Do reviewers report "the interface looks like early 90's", features "missing or restricted after payment", and "Google sheets… works much better than this" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.amura.selldo&hl=en_IN&showAllReviews=true)). That argues for **integrating with incumbent CRMs, not replacing them**.

The realistic threats are Sell.Do or Leadrat partnering with a 3D studio, Square Yards combining PropVR with its CRM and brokerage assets, and Bayut Studios bundling 3D with portal leads.

## Build the intent pipe first and the spectacle second

The Aurelle demo already holds more of the product than its "website" framing suggests:

- The INVENTORY config drives prices and availability in the explorer. That is the single source of truth a bot and CRM need, and it answers the "already sold" complaint.
- `extract-walls.py` turns 2D plans into 3D walls, which is the basis for a fast per-project template pipeline.
- The lead form already captures unit interest, budget, preferred contact, UTM parameters and a honeypot, and it hands off to WhatsApp with details pre-filled.

What is missing is everything that makes the product measurable and compliant:

- **Measurement.** There is no behavioural event stream tying a browsing session to a lead. Nothing captures Meta click identifiers for Conversions API. The `wa.me` link sends buyers to a personal number, so the conversation never reaches a system.
- **Qualification.** There are no timeline, purpose or financing fields, and no phone verification.
- **Compliance.** There is no Trakheesi permit, Madmoun QR or escrow block. The single bundled consent checkbox cannot serve as per-purpose evidence.

| Later | CP commission ledger and payout tracking; DPDP consent-manager hook; India/UAE hosting option | Payout delays; enterprise and government-linked developer demands | — | Vendor claims; DPDP timeline |

The bot's design follows from the evidence and the rules. It should answer only from structured, versioned facts. It must never let a language model generate a price, discount, possession date or return promise. It should log every answer for audit. It should open with a disclosure such as "I'm the AI assistant for <Developer>; an advisor can take over anytime". It should never request full ID, card or account numbers, sending KYC and token payments to a secure web link instead. The explorer should show "from" prices and live availability before the form. Kolonell claims a "from" price lifts the qualified rate 20-30% (vendor claim) ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/real-estate-developer-website-lead-capture-dubai-2026)). The detailed unit price sheet can sit behind a verified WhatsApp opt-in, which is itself a strong intent signal.

### Score on fit, intent and reachability, then let outcomes recalibrate

No independent study ranks which signals predict a real estate booking. The only independent test of a vendor's predictive real estate score found it was right about **1 in 5** times ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2019/03/26/revaluates-likely-to-move-score-is-right-1-in-5-times/)). Start with transparent rules that sales staff can read ("Hot: 2BHK 14-C viewed three times, budget fits, wants Saturday visit"). Use **attended site visit** as the training label and the Meta optimisation event: it happens well inside Meta's 28-day window, given ANAROCK's 26-day average lead-to-booking lag. The weights below are starting hypotheses to calibrate on each client's own outcomes.

## Twenty-six rules collapse into six product features

Compliance is where a startup can beat cheap bots and agencies outright, because most of the rules are mechanical and most competitors leave them to the client. The regulatory notes list 26 discrete requirements. They reduce to six features: jurisdiction-aware disclosure blocks, permit and registration gating with expiry tracking, a per-purpose consent ledger, a scoped and fact-grounded bot with human handoff, channel-specific outreach throttles, and retention and deletion tooling.

Three of these deserve emphasis because they change the product, not just the paperwork.

First, the **January 2026 WhatsApp rule** makes scope a feature. A property bot that answers anything is legally fragile. One that answers only about the client's projects and hands off fast is explicitly allowed.

Second, Dubai's rule that **ad content must not exceed what the permit approved** means the bot's knowledge base must be the permitted content. Its answers are advertising.

Third, RERA Section 12 lets an Indian buyer misled by an advertisement withdraw with a full refund and interest ([Housing.com](https://housing.com/news/what-is-the-promoters-responsibility-as-per-section-12-of-the-rera-act)). Combine that with the Air Canada ruling, and a hallucinated possession date becomes a refund liability.

These rules are a sales argument. "Every page, ad and bot answer is permit-checked and logged" addresses a risk that developers carry personally. Dubai brokers say "Gone are the days of running ads freely or making claims without evidence" ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-property-agents-under-pressure-as-real-estate-market-heats-up-in-2025-1.500167818)).

## Price per launch and prove it per site visit

Clients already buy every component separately, which sets the anchors:

- **CRM seats:** about ₹1,000-3,000 per user per month in India; PropSpace at AED 176-220 per user in Dubai.
- **Bots:** custom WhatsApp qualification bots at about ₹1.25-1.75 lakh to build plus ₹5,000-25,000 a month ([Brainguru](https://www.brainguru.in/blog/2026/04/whatsapp-chatbot-development-cost-india/)).
- **3D:** cinematic building tours at ₹1-2.5 lakh ([Arcnviz](https://arcnviz.pages.dev/blog/virtual-tours-3d-walkthrough-kolkata-real-estate)).
- **Developer sites:** in the Gulf, USD 12,000-30,000 per site plus USD 800-2,000 for each added project ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/real-estate-developer-website-lead-generation-cost-riyadh-2026)).
- **Agencies:** retainers of AED 5,000-50,000 a month in Dubai.

Developers budget marketing per project and front-load it around launch. The vendor-sourced figure is 1.5-4% of projected sales value; the exact source page is uncertain and the figure is unverified against annual reports ([Sell.Do](https://www.sell.do/blog/marketing-strategies-for-real-estate-developers-and-brokers)). In Dubai, spend concentrates in a 2-3 week launch window ([media.co.uk](https://media.co.uk/blogs/blog/al-rabia-real-estate-developers-property-launch-campaigns)). Buyers prefer 90-day single-project pilots ([UrbanHouzz](https://urbanhouzz-website.pages.dev/)). The pricing below follows those patterns. The figures are hypotheses for interviews, not market quotes.

The ROI story should be stated in the client's own numbers, never as "10x". On Kolonell's modelled AED 480,000 Dubai launch budget ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/real-estate-developer-website-lead-capture-dubai-2026)), Meta's platform-reported 15-21% lower cost per quality lead from CRM feedback would be worth roughly AED 70,000-100,000 per launch, which covers the pilot. Shifting a single booking from broker-sourced to provably direct saves AED 72,000-108,000. Neither is guaranteed. The pilot's job is to measure cost per attended visit before and after.

**For acquiring clients on Meta, the bot is the ad and the demo is the offer.** Meta's B2B targeting has eroded. Detailed-targeting exclusions were removed in 2025 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-removes-detailed-targeting-exclusions-for-ad-accounts/)), and more interest categories were reportedly cut in January 2026 ([AdLibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-interest-targeting-removed)). Audience quality therefore has to come from three places:

1. **Custom audiences** built from public registries (state RERA developer lists, DLD broker and brokerage records) and expo exhibitor lists such as Cityscape's roughly 595 exhibitors ([ExhibitorsData](https://exhibitorsdata.com/product/cityscape-global-exhibitor-list/)). Check DPDP and PDPL before uploading personal data.
2. **Lookalikes and video-engager retargeting** built from those audiences.
3. **Creative that self-selects**: only property professionals stop for a scroll-film of a tower with live unit availability.

Run click-to-WhatsApp ads into the founder's own bot. Vendor India B2B figures put click-to-WhatsApp at ₹200-800 per lead against ₹400-1,500 for lead forms ([Sotros](https://sotrosinfotech.pages.dev/blog/whatsapp-click-to-chat-ads-b2b-lead-generation-2026/)). The prospect then experiences the speed, scoping and scoring first-hand.

The strongest positioning line is **"Lower cost per site visit on your next launch"**. Supporting lines are "every enquiry answered in 60 seconds, in permit-approved words" and "know who brought the buyer before the dispute starts".

The offers, in likely order of strength:

1. **"Send one floor plan; see your unit in 3D in 72 hours"**, which the plan-to-walls tool makes cheap.
2. **A lead-leakage audit**: enquire on the prospect's live ads at 9:47 PM on a Friday and report the response time and channel.
3. **A cost-per-site-visit calculator** run inside the bot.
4. **"Live before your launch window"**: a fixed go-live date with part of the setup fee at risk.

Avoid "guaranteed bookings", "₹50 leads", "10x ROI" and generic "AI-powered", which the cost-per-booking framing has already discredited.

Two channel partners fit naturally. CGI and visualisation studios already hold the 3D assets and developer relationships but lack a web, bot or CRM layer. Performance agencies are judged on cost per booking and would gain from better conversion. Both partnerships are inference; no case studies were found.

One caution on the demo itself. Aurelle is fictional and priced in AED. If ad creative showing it reaches Dubai consumers, it could be read as property advertising without a Trakheesi permit. Label it plainly as a concept demo and target it at professional audiences. This is an inference to confirm with a Dubai advisor.

## What could break the thesis, and ten interview questions that test it

**Evidence risk.** Every number that justifies the product's uplift is a vendor claim: qualified share, visit rates, bot-driven visit increases, and the claim that 3D raises lead quality. The founder should assume none of the uplifts and design pilots to measure them, with a baseline period, the same media spend and cost per attended visit as the metric.

**Positioning risk.** Brokers source most primary sales in both markets, and the top few percent of CPs carry the volume. A product pitched as "cut out the brokers" will alienate the sales heads who must champion it. "Attribute fairly and equip your best brokers" is safer and still captures the commission economics.

**Services-trap risk.** Premium 3D per project is agency economics unless the plan-to-3D pipeline, inventory config and scroll-film structure become a template that ships a new project in days.

**Platform risk.** WhatsApp pricing changed three times in 2025-26. The AI-provider ban shows Meta will redraw lines. Per-user marketing caps limit nurture. Meta targeting keeps shrinking.

**Liability risk.** A bot misquoting price or possession is a RERA Section 12 and consumer-law exposure. The fact-locked design is mandatory, not optional.

**Adoption risk.** Sales teams fall back to Sheets and WhatsApp when tools are clunky. Scores must arrive where reps already work.

**Data risk.** DPDP core duties arrive in May 2027. UAE PDPL timing is unclear. Sending chat text to a foreign LLM is a cross-border transfer that government-linked Dubai developers may refuse.

| # | Hypothesis | Ask whom | Question | What would falsify it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Owners track cost per visit or booking, not CPL | Developer MD, sales head | "What did your last launch cost per site visit, and how do you know?" | They cannot answer and do not care to |
| 2 | Most Meta leads are unreachable or out of budget, not fake | Pre-sales head | "Of your last 100 Meta leads, how many answered, fit budget, and visited?" | Reachability is fine; visits fail for other reasons |
| 3 | A large share of enquiries arrive after hours | Pre-sales head, brokerage owner | "What share of leads arrive after 7 PM or on weekends, and when is first contact?" | First contact is already under 15 minutes around the clock |
| 4 | Developers will let a bot answer price and availability | MD, legal or compliance | "Who approves what the bot may say? Would you allow live prices?" | Legal will not permit any automated price answers |
| 5 | Integration beats replacement | CRM owner | "Which CRM, who owns the WhatsApp numbers, and would you switch?" | They demand a full CRM replacement or refuse any new data flow |
| 6 | CP disputes are frequent and costly | Sales head, top CPs | "How many disputed bookings last quarter, and how were they settled? Would brokers use tagged links?" | Disputes are rare or settled informally at no cost |
| 7 | Exploring units in 3D predicts visit quality | Sales team in pilot | Compare visit-to-booking for leads with and without deep explorer use | No difference after 50-100 visits |
| 8 | Budget comes from launch marketing, signed by the MD, decided 4-8 weeks before launch | MD, CMO | "Which budget would pay for this, who signs, and when do you lock launch vendors?" | Procurement cycles run past the launch window |
| 9 | Overseas and NRI buyers are a large, under-served share | Sales head | "What share of bookings are overseas, which time zones and languages, and how many book without visiting?" | Overseas share is small or handled by brokers abroad |
| 10 | Pricing of setup plus monthly is acceptable | MD | "Would you pay [setup] + [monthly] for a 90-day pilot measured on cost per attended visit?" | Only pure performance or revenue share is acceptable |

## Conclusion

The research changes what the product is. The 3D scroll-film is the least-evidenced part of the bundle. Buyers rate floor plans above virtual tours, and no study ties 3D to off-plan lead quality. Yet it is the only part that creates data no competitor has. Treat the 3D site as an instrument whose output is a score, and treat the score as the thing that feeds Meta's optimiser and settles who introduced the buyer. That turns a commoditised trio (pretty site, WhatsApp bot, CRM) into a product competitors cannot copy by adding a feature. It also points the commercial argument at commission and cost per visit, where the money is, rather than at CPL, where agencies already compete on price.

The second shift is sequencing. Dubai off-plan developers combine many launches, overseas buyers, weak developer-CRM incumbents and AED budgets, and the founder's demo is already Dubai-native. A first year spent winning a dozen Dubai launches with pilots measured on attended visits will produce something the market lacks entirely: independent-quality evidence. That evidence would be the strongest sales asset for India's larger but more price-sensitive premium segment. The founder's first ten interviews should aim to falsify hypotheses 1, 2, 4 and 7 in the table above. If those four survive, the roadmap holds.
